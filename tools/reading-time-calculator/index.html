How Reading Time Is Actually Calculated

Every "5 min read" badge on the web comes from the same arithmetic: divide the word count by a reading speed expressed in words per minute. The interesting part is not the division, it is the speed you pick, because that single number decides whether the badge is honest.

The formula

reading time (minutes) = word count / words per minute

1,000 words / 200 wpm = 5 minutes
1,000 words / 130 wpm = 7.7 minutes   (spoken aloud)
1,000 words / 400 wpm = 2.5 minutes   (skimmed)

Most platforms round up to the nearest whole minute, so a 4.2 minute article displays as "5 min read". Rounding up is the safe direction: a reader who finishes early is pleasantly surprised, a reader who is misled stops trusting the badge.

Research on silent reading in English typically lands between 200 and 250 words per minute for general prose. Two hundred has become the default in blogging platforms because it is a conservative middle value: it flatters neither the author nor the reader.

Why badges disagree between sites

Two sites publishing the identical article can show different read times, and neither is broken. Medium divides by roughly 265 words per minute and adds seconds for each inline image. WordPress plugins commonly use 200 or 250. Some count headings and captions as words, others do not. The honest move is to pick one convention and apply it consistently across your own site.

Speaking time is a different number

People speak far more slowly than they read. A conversational pace is around 130 words per minute, and conference talks often run slower because of pauses, demos and slides. If you are writing for the ear rather than the eye, use the speaking column and not the reading one.

Where the formula breaks

  • Chinese, Japanese and Korean. These scripts have no spaces, so a word count is meaningless. Silent reading speed in Chinese is measured in characters per minute, commonly 300 to 500. This calculator shows a separate CJK character count for that reason.
  • Code, tables and equations. A page that is half code blocks is not read at prose speed. Nobody reads 200 words of SQL per minute.
  • Images and video. A word count cannot see them. If your page leads with a two-minute video, add it yourself.
  • Scanning, not reading. Many visitors never read linearly at all. A badge is a promise about the committed reader, nothing more.
Practical tip: measure one of your own articles with a stopwatch once. Whatever number you get, use it as your site-wide speed instead of the default, and your badges will be more honest than most.

Frequently asked questions

How is reading time calculated?

The word count divided by a reading speed in words per minute. At 200 wpm, a 1,000-word article takes about five minutes.

Why 200 words per minute?

Silent reading in English is usually measured between 200 and 250 wpm for general prose. Two hundred is the conservative middle value most platforms adopted.

Is Medium's read time different?

Yes. It uses roughly 265 wpm and adds time for inline images, so its numbers come out shorter than a plain word-count estimate.

Does it work for Chinese or Japanese?

No. Those scripts have no spaces. Use characters per minute instead, typically 300 to 500 for Chinese.

How many words for a five-minute video?

About 650 words at a conversational 130 wpm, or about 750 at 150 wpm.

Can I use this for presentation timing?

Yes. Set the slider to 130 wpm and rehearse once against a stopwatch, because individual pace varies more than any formula captures.

Does this upload my text?

No. It is pure client-side JavaScript and your draft never leaves your browser tab.
